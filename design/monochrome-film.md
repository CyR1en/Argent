# Tri-X 400 and HP5 Plus: published data and model assumptions

No film or print scans were used. This path is a published-data approximation, not a calibrated scanner or a claim that one film always has higher contrast.

RGB proxies use three equal-weight wavelengths per working channel: red 590/610/630 nm, green 510/550/580 nm, blue 450/470/490 nm. Kodak's log sensitivities are converted to linear before integration. Filter values below the table's reported transmission are treated as zero. The channel bands are not Sony sensor sensitivities and do not reconstruct scene spectra; in particular, the uncorrected Ilford curve is for the stated tungsten test source.

The characteristic curves are sampled at half-log-exposure intervals, re-centered at an estimated exposure for scene mid-gray, extrapolated by the last slope, and bounded by the negative LUT's density range. The chosen Tri-X development gives a steeper midtone response than the HP5 Plus reference. Auto keeps the existing scene exposure and detail analysis; its print predictions use the selected spectral mix and paper grade. B&W does not apply color cast corrections.

The negative LUT retains 2.40 density units of storage; this is not the paper's exposure range. Relative to the gray anchor, a denser silver negative reduces log paper exposure one-for-one. For grade 2, the modeled 0.90-log-exposure interval runs from near black (90% of the modeled density above base) to near white (0.04 density above base), with smooth tails. The former smoothstep spread its transition across the entire negative LUT and left diffuse whites much too low. The new mapping leaves the negative, the Auto aim and the color-stock path unchanged.

Measured on the public Sony a7 IV frame DSC06673, with both versions exported as linear Rec. 2020 converted to sRGB, the sunlit white wall's median linear luminance rose from 0.503 to 0.913 on Tri-X and from 0.456 to 0.871 on HP5 Plus. The new wall's 10th–90th percentiles were 0.880–0.939 and 0.831–0.905 respectively; Argent Neutral's median stayed 0.757. The beige wall midtone moved from 0.296 to 0.539 and 0.287 to 0.509 (Neutral 0.405). This is an image check, not paper sensitometry. The original contact-sheet helper also incorrectly tagged linear texture data as sRGB; the same correction was applied to both sides of this comparison, using the app export's linear working-space tag.
